PEFT methods should be evaluated by target gains, general-capability retention, and the geometry of capability disruption.

PEFT-Arena studies PEFT through a single diagnostic chain: external stability-plasticity trade-offs, internal geometry, and pathwise interpolation.

TL;DR

PEFT evaluation should ask not only what improves, but also what is forgotten and why. PEFT-Arena benchmarks target adaptation and general retention, then traces forgetting back to weight-space update geometry, activation-space distortion, and interpolation paths.

Main message. Target-only evaluation is incomplete. The same target score can hide very different levels of general-capability retention, and those differences are tied to how each PEFT parameterization perturbs pretrained geometry.

Why target accuracy is not enough

Parameter-efficient finetuning is often evaluated by a single downstream number. That misses the stability-plasticity dilemma: a model can learn a target domain while eroding instruction following, factual recall, or broad reasoning. PEFT-Arena makes this trade-off explicit by reporting target-domain adaptation together with retained general capability.

01 Target-only evaluation is incomplete.

Improving math or medical reasoning is not sufficient if the model breaks general abilities.

02 Retention is a first-class metric.

We evaluate general capability with IFEval, NQ, and BBH alongside target-domain scores.

03 Parameterization matters.

Full FT, LoRA-style updates, PiSSA, MiLoRA, OFT, and IA3 produce different frontiers.

Internal geometry: where forgetting comes from

Benchmark scores say what changed, but not how. PEFT-Arena therefore analyzes PEFT updates from two internal views. In weight space, spectral profiles describe how update energy interacts with pretrained singular directions. In activation space, Procrustes residual, Gram distortion, and CKA test whether finetuning preserves the relational geometry of general-evaluation representations.

The key distinction is that raw movement is not always destructive. OFT can move activations through rotation-like transformations, while preserving more of their relational structure. Forgetting is more closely tied to non-isometric distortion of general representations than to displacement alone.

Three diagnostics. Weight profiles describe update bias; capability-conditioned drift measures how strongly general and target data activate changed directions; activation geometry measures non-isometric distortion with Procrustes, Gram, and CKA.

Interpolation as pathwise diagnosis

A final checkpoint is only one point on an adaptation path. Interpolation asks whether the model moved farther than necessary. In SFT, intermediate points often recover general ability while preserving much of the target gain, revealing an overshoot region that final-checkpoint evaluation hides.

The interpolation path must match the parameterization. Full FT, LoRA, and PiSSA naturally interpolate by scaling the effective update ΔW. OFT should instead follow its Cayley-generator path; dense-weight interpolation leaves the orthogonal manifold. Layer-wise OFT rewinding is a post-hoc case study of controlling imbalanced update strength along an existing finetuning path.
